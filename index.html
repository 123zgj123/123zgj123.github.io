Hallucination as Exploit: Evidence-Carrying Multimodal Agents

Guijia Zhang, Hao Zheng, Harry Yang

Findings of EMNLP 2026 · First author · Evidence & authorization

A hallucinated observation can become a security failure when an agent treats it as permission to use a tool. ECA decomposes tool calls into action-critical predicates, verifies typed DOM, OCR, and accessibility evidence, and applies a deterministic authorization gate before execution. Across 1,700 tests spanning 17 attack categories, the hardened gate recorded zero canonical bypasses.

STARS: Skill Triage via Activation Risk Scoring for LLM Agents

Guijia Zhang, Shu Yang, Xilin Gong, Di Wang

Findings of EMNLP 2026 · First author · Invocation-time risk

The risk of invoking a skill depends on the current request, so a tool-level assessment alone is not enough. STARS combines a static capability prior, a request-conditioned risk scorer, and calibrated fusion to allocate a limited review budget across skill activations. On the 3,000-record SIA-Bench, held-out high-risk AUPRC improves by 12% over the strongest proxy baseline.

When Token-Level Divergence Misranks Prompt-Injection Robustness

Under Review · First author · Evaluation validity

Token-level divergence need not track whether a prompt injection changes an agent’s action. Controlled experiments in masked diffusion language models expose a counterexample where lower clean-to-injected KL divergence accompanies more successful attacks. The analysis separates numerical sensitivity from decision relevance and establishes margin conditions under which stability on relevant tokens preserves an authorized action.

Rollback as Authority Revocation: Binding Caches to Committed History

Under Review · First author · State provenance

Removing a branch from a conversation does not necessarily revoke its influence if later decisions reuse that branch’s KV-cache state. This work defines a provenance contract binding KV-cache reuse to committed history and shows that retained prefixes change authorization outcomes in 43 of 63 tested settings across seven model families. In these tests, a reference adapter restores authorized actions in all 36 edited histories and preserves them in 18 no-op controls.

When Can Historical Scores Be Reused After Judge Replacement?

Under Review · First author · Judge replacement

When an LLM judge is replaced, historical scores do not automatically support the same downstream decisions. This study separates decision compatibility, finite-population certification, and economic usefulness to derive an audit-budget frontier. Across 18 previously recorded gateway comparisons, certified reuse saves under 3% of incoming calls at 10% tolerance and the original audit budget, while failed audits can increase rescoring costs.

Do GUI Agents Believe Their Eyes? Diagnosing State-Belief Reliance on Pixels versus Structure

Guijia Zhang, Yuxun Chen, Yuheng Qi, Harry Yang

Under Review · First author · Perception & belief

GUI agents observe both screenshots and structured accessibility or DOM trees, creating ambiguity when the two channels conflict. Paired single-channel interventions across 735 web, mobile, and desktop probes disentangle visual perception from downstream state belief. Across seven evaluated models, agents frequently defer to structured text even when they accurately perceive the contradicting pixels, yielding a Perception-Fusion Gap of 0.30 to 0.75.

The Commitment Window: An Operator-Relative Late Override Span under Conflicting Evidence

Under Review · Second author · Causal interventions

This study asks when a model’s preference between conflicting evidence chains becomes resistant to a specified causal intervention. Layer-wise donor patching across nine models identifies a narrow late-layer commitment window defined relative to the intervention operator. An effective intervention layer is found in 83% of gated conflict pairs, compared with 1.7% at neutral control positions.

Agent Skill Regulation as a Procedural Knowledge Representation Problem

Under Review · Co-first author (2nd listed) · Procedural knowledge

Human-written skills describe workflows in prose, leaving execution constraints implicit during agent runs. A syntax–semantics–execution representation aligns operation and object flows and combines formal priors with procedural evidence through bidirectional optimization. Runtime regulation improves task success by 4.3 percentage points and reduces erroneous submissions by 37.2 percentage points.

Research software

I build tools that keep research proposals separate from experimental results. PaperFuture helps develop testable ideas from the literature; FarField-v2 tracks experiments, competing explanations, and the evidence behind each conclusion.

PaperFuture

GitHub ↗

Literature-grounded research ideation and branch-isolated synthesis

PaperFuture connects papers, proposed mechanisms, and experiment plans while keeping alternative hypotheses separate. Its versioned corpora cover four research areas, with 25,059 deduplicated paper versions and 568 full-text papers.

  • Branch-isolated memory: Retains competing explanations and reviewer disagreements across multi-model conditional simulations instead of collapsing prematurely to a consensus idea.
  • Reproducible corpus snapshots: Enforces frozen literature snapshots, token budget accounting, resumable state, and deterministic offline replay.

FarField-v2

GitHub ↗

Evidence-gated automated research runtime

FarField-v2 coordinates hypotheses, experiments, competing explanations, and review. It records which experiment supports each claim and checks provenance and reproduction before treating a generated proposal as a result.

  • Preregistered two-arm probes: Binds proposed claims to concrete scientific objects and baseline comparisons before execution begins.
  • Reproduction gates: Separates model-generated proposals from measured empirical outcomes, auditing failures through offline regression suites and bounded live runs.

World models & capability development

I am exploring whether models of capability development could help plan training for verifiable code and mathematics tasks. The idea is to represent current abilities as a state and a curriculum as an action, then study how learning depends on data coverage and reliable feedback. This remains an exploratory direction.
